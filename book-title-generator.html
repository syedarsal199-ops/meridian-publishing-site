    What makes a book title work

    A title has one job on a retail page: make a stranger stop scrolling and read the subtitle. It does not have to summarise the book. The best-selling non-fiction titles of the last decade are mostly two or three words that promise a feeling, with a subtitle doing the explaining underneath.

    Four things to test before you commit. Say it out loud — if you stumble, readers will too. Search it on Amazon — if three books share your title in the same category, you will lose the ranking fight. Check it as a domain and a handle, because you will want both. And read it as a recommendation: “you should read [your title]” should sound like something a person would actually say.

    For memoir, specificity beats abstraction — a concrete image travels further than an emotion. For business, the promise should be visible in the title itself. For fiction, the title sets tone before the cover does, and a title that sounds like three other books in your genre is doing its job rather than failing at originality.
