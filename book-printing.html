Book Printing & Fulfilment

Print-on-demand suits most authors. Offset suits authors with a reason to hold inventory. Choosing wrongly either ties up your money or costs you margin on every copy.

The approach

The question is not which is better. It is how many copies you can actually sell.

Print-on-demand means no inventory, no upfront cost, and a higher cost per copy. Offset printing means a real investment and a garage full of books, but a unit cost low enough to make bulk sales and bookstore margins work.

Below roughly 250 copies, print-on-demand almost always wins. Above it, offset starts to pay — and if you are speaking at events or selling to corporate buyers, it pays considerably.

What is included

  • Honest print-on-demand versus offset cost modelling
  • Paper stock, weight and finish selection with physical samples
  • A free physical proof before any run is authorised
  • Special finishes: foil, emboss, spot gloss, deckle edge
  • Hardcover with dust jacket, or case-laminate binding
  • Direct fulfilment to events, bulk buyers or your door
  • Reprint management as stock runs down
