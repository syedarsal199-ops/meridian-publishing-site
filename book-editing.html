Book Editing & Proofreading

Editing is three different jobs that get called one word. Knowing which one your manuscript actually needs saves you both money and a great deal of disappointment.

The approach

Most manuscripts do not need a proofreader. They need someone to tell them chapter four should be chapter one.

A developmental edit interrogates structure: what order the material belongs in, which chapters earn their place, where the argument sags. A line edit works sentence by sentence on rhythm and clarity. A copy edit and proofread catch the errors. They are sequential, and doing them out of order wastes money.

We will read a sample and tell you which you need. If your manuscript only needs a proofread, we will say that rather than selling you three passes.

What is included

  • Sample read and honest recommendation before you buy
  • Developmental edit with a full editorial letter
  • Line edit for voice, rhythm and clarity
  • Copy edit against Chicago style, or your house style
  • Final proofread on typeset pages, not just the manuscript
  • A scheduled call with your editor to talk through the letter
  • Style sheet documenting every decision made
